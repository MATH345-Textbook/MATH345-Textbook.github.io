The projection theorem, Theorem 4.2.7. If q one through q m is an orthonormal basis of a subspace U, then the projection of x onto U is the sum of the terms x dot q i, times q i.

Take Example 4.2.11. U is the span of f one and f two, and x is two, zero, one. f one and f two are orthogonal, each with length root two, so normalize them to get an orthonormal basis, q one and q two.

Project x onto the line spanned by q one. The coefficient x dot q one is root two, so this piece is root two times q one: one, one, zero.

Likewise, x dot q two is root two, giving one, negative one, zero.

Now add the pieces. The projection of x onto U is two, zero, zero. The residual v, x minus p, is zero, zero, one. It is orthogonal to q one and q two, so it lies in U perp.

So: find an orthonormal basis of U, project x onto each basis vector, and add.
