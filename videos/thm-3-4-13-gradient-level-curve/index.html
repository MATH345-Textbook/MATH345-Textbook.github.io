Theorem 3.4.13. If f has continuous partial derivatives and a nonzero gradient at a, b, then the gradient is perpendicular to the level curve through a, b. Here's why.

Continuous partial derivatives make f differentiable at a, b. Let c equal f of a, b. By Theorem 3.4.11, the tangent plane to the graph at a, b, c has this equation.

Along the level curve f of x, y equals c, the height is constant, so the lifted curve lies in the horizontal plane z equals c. If v is a tangent direction to the level curve, the lifted curve has tangent direction v one, v two, zero.

Its tangent line, r of t, passes through a, b, c, with direction vector v one, v two, zero, and it lies in the tangent plane.

So x equals a plus t v one, y equals b plus t v two, and z equals c satisfy the plane equation. Substituting and subtracting c leaves zero equals t times this sum.

For t not zero, divide by t. The gradient of f at a, b, dot v, equals zero. So the gradient is perpendicular to the level curve's tangent direction.
