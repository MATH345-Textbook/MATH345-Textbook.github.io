Activity 3.5.23. A tiny sigmoid block (U3-LO5, U3-LO6, U3-LO7).

Let
\begin{equation*} W_1= \begin{bmatrix} 1&0\\ 0&1\\ 1&1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad W_2= \begin{bmatrix} 1&0&-1/2\\ 0&1&1/2 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
and let \(\mathbf{b}_1=\mathbf{0}\) and \(\mathbf{b}_2=\mathbf{0}\text{.}\)
🔗
Define
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{s}=W_1\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{b}_1, \qquad \mathbf{u}=\sigma(\mathbf{s}), \qquad N(\mathbf{x})=W_2\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{b}_2, \end{equation*}
where \(\sigma\) is applied coordinatewise. For each neuron \(i=1,2,3\) in the first layer, \(s_i\) is its weighted sum plus bias, and \(u_i=\sigma(s_i)\) is its output after applying the sigmoid function. The second affine map combines these three outputs.
🔗

Inputs x 1 and x 2 are on the left, three hidden neurons labeled sigma are in the middle, and outputs N 1 and N 2 are on the right. Each hidden neuron i takes s i to u i. Each edge is labeled by an entry of W 1 or W 2. From the inputs: x 1 to neuron 1 with weight 1, x 2 to neuron 2 with weight 1, and both x 1 and x 2 to neuron 3 with weight 1. The two edges into neuron 3 are highlighted in red; their weights form row 3 of W 1, the row 1, 1. To the outputs: neuron 1 to N 1 with weight 1, neuron 3 to N 1 with weight minus one half, neuron 2 to N 2 with weight 1, and neuron 3 to N 2 with weight one half. A missing edge corresponds to a zero entry of W 1 or W 2. Below the network, an arrow says data flows left to right, and the local matrices of the three steps are listed in order: W 1, D a, W 2. The Jacobian matrix is J N of a equals W 2 times D a times W 1, so the matrices multiply right to left.
🔗
At the base point
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{a} = \begin{bmatrix} 0\\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
do the following.
🔗
  1. Compute the pre-activation vector \(\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}}=W_1\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}_1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Compute the hidden vector \(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}=\sigma(\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Compute \(N(\mathbf{a})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Use \(\sigma'(0)=1/4\) to compute
    \begin{equation*} D_{\mathbf{a}} = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(\sigma'((\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}})_1),\sigma'((\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}})_2),\sigma'((\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}})_3)\bigr). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Use the chain rule to justify and compute
    \begin{equation*} J_N(\mathbf{a})=W_2D_{\mathbf{a}}W_1. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  6. Use
    \begin{equation*} N(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{h})\approx N(\mathbf{a})+J_N(\mathbf{a})\mathbf{h} \end{equation*}
    to predict the output change caused by a small input change \(\mathbf{h}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
Solution.
Since \(\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{0}\) and \(\mathbf{b}_1=\mathbf{0}\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}} = W_1\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0\\ 0\\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
🔗
Therefore
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}} = \sigma(\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}}) = \begin{bmatrix} 1/2\\ 1/2\\ 1/2 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Since \(\mathbf{b}_2=\mathbf{0}\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} N(\mathbf{a}) = W_2\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1&0&-1/2\\ 0&1&1/2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1/2\\ 1/2\\ 1/2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/4\\ 3/4 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
To derive the Jacobian formula, write the three steps as maps:
\begin{align*} F_1(\mathbf{x}) \amp=W_1\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{b}_1,\\ S(\mathbf{s}) \amp=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma(s_1)\\\sigma(s_2)\\\sigma(s_3)\end{bmatrix},\\ F_2(\mathbf{u}) \amp=W_2\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{b}_2. \end{align*}
Then \(N=F_2\circ S\circ F_1\text{,}\) with \(F_1(\mathbf{a})=\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}}\) and \(S(\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}})=\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}\text{.}\)
🔗
Differentiating the affine maps gives
\begin{equation*} J_{F_1}(\mathbf{x})=W_1,\qquad J_{F_2}(\mathbf{u})=W_2, \end{equation*}
because the bias vectors are constant. For the middle map, \(S_i(\mathbf{s})=\sigma(s_i)\) depends only on \(s_i\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} \frac{\partial S_i}{\partial s_j}(\mathbf{s})= \begin{cases}\sigma'(s_i),\amp i=j,\\0,\amp i\ne j.\end{cases} \end{equation*}
Thus
\begin{equation*} J_S(\mathbf{s})= \begin{bmatrix}\sigma'(s_1)\amp0\amp0\\0\amp\sigma'(s_2)\amp0\\0\amp0\amp\sigma'(s_3)\end{bmatrix}, \qquad J_S(\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}})=D_{\mathbf{a}}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Applying the chain rule first to \(F_2\circ(S\circ F_1)\) and then to \(S\circ F_1\) gives
\begin{align*} J_N(\mathbf{a}) \amp=J_{F_2}\bigl(S(F_1(\mathbf{a}))\bigr)J_{S\circ F_1}(\mathbf{a})\\ \amp=J_{F_2}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})J_S(\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}})J_{F_1}(\mathbf{a})\\ \amp=W_2D_{\mathbf{a}}W_1. \end{align*}
The matrix shapes are \((2\times3)(3\times3)(3\times2)\text{,}\) giving a \(2\times2\) Jacobian. The rightmost factor acts first on the input change.
🔗
At our base point, all entries of \(\mathbf{s}_{\mathbf{a}}\) are zero. Since \(\sigma'(0)=1/4\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} D_{\mathbf{a}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1/4&0&0\\ 0&1/4&0\\ 0&0&1/4 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Substituting into the chain-rule formula,
\begin{align*} J_N(\mathbf{a})\amp=W_2D_{\mathbf{a}}W_1\\ \amp=\begin{bmatrix}1\amp0\amp-1/2\\0\amp1\amp1/2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1/4\amp0\amp0\\0\amp1/4\amp0\\0\amp0\amp1/4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1\amp0\\0\amp1\\1\amp1\end{bmatrix}\\ \amp=\begin{bmatrix}1/8\amp-1/8\\1/8\amp3/8\end{bmatrix}. \end{align*}
🔗
\begin{equation*} N(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{h}) \approx \begin{bmatrix} 1/4\\ 3/4 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 1/8&-1/8\\ 1/8&3/8 \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{h}. \end{equation*}
This gives the intended local prediction: the Jacobian matrix of the composition is a product of local matrices.
