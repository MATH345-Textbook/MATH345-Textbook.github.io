Theorem 3.4.12.
Let \(f(x,y)\) be a function with continuous partial derivatives. If \((a,b)\) is a point where \(\nabla f(a,b) \neq \mathbf{0}\text{,}\) then \(\nabla f(a,b)\) is perpendicular to the level curve of \(f\) passing through the point \((a,b)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Why is this true?.
By Theorem 3.4.6, the continuous partial derivatives make \(f\) differentiable at \((a,b)\text{.}\) Let \(c=f(a,b)\text{.}\) By Theorem 3.4.10, the tangent plane to the graph of \(f\) at \((a,b,c)\) has equation
\begin{equation*} z-c=\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}(a,b)(x-a)+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}(a,b)(y-b). \end{equation*}
🔗
Along the level curve \(f(x,y)=c\text{,}\) the height is constant. Lifting this curve onto the graph of \(f\) therefore places it entirely in the horizontal plane \(z=c\text{.}\) If \(\mathbf{v}=(v_1,v_2)\) is a nonzero tangent direction to the level curve at \((a,b)\text{,}\) then the lifted curve has tangent direction \((v_1,v_2,0)\text{:}\) there is no vertical change.
🔗
The tangent line to this lifted curve lies in the tangent plane. Substituting a point \((a+tv_1,b+tv_2,c)\) on that tangent line into the plane equation gives
\begin{equation*} 0=t\left(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}(a,b)v_1+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}(a,b)v_2\right). \end{equation*}
For \(t\ne 0\text{,}\) dividing by \(t\) yields
\begin{equation*} \nabla f(a,b)\cdot\mathbf{v} =\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}(a,b)v_1+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}(a,b)v_2=0. \end{equation*}
A zero dot product means that the gradient is perpendicular to the tangent direction of the level curve.
