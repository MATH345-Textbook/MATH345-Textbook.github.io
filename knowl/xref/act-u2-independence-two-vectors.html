Activity 2.4.4. Independence of two vectors (U2-LO4).

Let \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\) be two nonzero vectors in \(\R^n\text{.}\) Explain why \(\{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\}\) is linearly independent if and only if neither vector is a scalar multiple of the other.
🔗
Solution.
If one vector is a scalar multiple of the other, say \(\mathbf{v}=c\mathbf{u}\text{,}\) then
\begin{equation*} c\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}. \end{equation*}
The coefficients \(c\) and \(-1\) are not both zero, so the vectors are linearly dependent. The same reasoning applies if \(\mathbf{u}\) is a scalar multiple of \(\mathbf{v}\text{.}\)
🔗
Conversely, suppose the vectors are linearly dependent. Then
\begin{equation*} s\mathbf{u}+t\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0} \end{equation*}
for scalars \(s\) and \(t\) that are not both zero. We must have \(t\ne 0\text{:}\) otherwise \(s\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}\text{,}\) and since \(\mathbf{u}\ne\mathbf{0}\text{,}\) this would force \(s=0\) as well. Therefore
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{v}=-\frac{s}{t}\mathbf{u}, \end{equation*}
so one vector is a scalar multiple of the other. Thus, if neither vector is a scalar multiple of the other, the only solution is \(s=t=0\text{,}\) and the vectors are linearly independent.
