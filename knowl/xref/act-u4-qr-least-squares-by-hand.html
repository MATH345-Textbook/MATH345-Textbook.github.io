Activity 4.4.11. Solving least squares with \(QR\) (U4-LO2, U4-LO6).

Use \(QR\) to fit a line \(y=c_0+c_1t\) to the data points
\begin{equation*} (0,1),\qquad(1,2),\qquad(2,2) \end{equation*}
from Activity 4.3.10. The design matrix and data vector are
\begin{equation*} X=\begin{bmatrix} 1\amp0\\ 1\amp1\\ 1\amp2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{y}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\\2\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
  1. Apply Gram–Schmidt to the columns of \(X\) to find a reduced factorization \(X=QR\) with positive diagonal entries in \(R\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Compute \(Q^T\mathbf{y}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Solve \(R\widehat{\mathbf{c}}=Q^T\mathbf{y}\) by back-substitution and write the fitted line.
    🔗
    🔗
Solution.
Write \(X=[\mathbf{a}_1\ \mathbf{a}_2]\text{.}\) The first column gives
\begin{equation*} r_{11}=\|\mathbf{a}_1\|=\sqrt{3}, \qquad \mathbf{q}_1=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
For the second column,
\begin{equation*} r_{12}=\mathbf{q}_1^T\mathbf{a}_2 =\frac{0+1+2}{\sqrt{3}}=\sqrt{3}. \end{equation*}
Subtracting its component along \(\mathbf{q}_1\) gives
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{v}_2 =\mathbf{a}_2-r_{12}\mathbf{q}_1 =\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\\2\end{bmatrix} -\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}-1\\0\\1\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Hence
\begin{equation*} r_{22}=\|\mathbf{v}_2\|=\sqrt{2}, \qquad \mathbf{q}_2=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}-1\\0\\1\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Thus the reduced factors are
\begin{equation*} Q=\begin{bmatrix} 1/\sqrt{3}\amp-1/\sqrt{2}\\ 1/\sqrt{3}\amp0\\ 1/\sqrt{3}\amp1/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad R=\begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{3}\amp\sqrt{3}\\ 0\amp\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
The transformed right-hand side is
\begin{equation*} Q^T\mathbf{y} =\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{q}_1^T\mathbf{y}\\ \mathbf{q}_2^T\mathbf{y} \end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix} (1+2+2)/\sqrt{3}\\ (-1+0+2)/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix} 5/\sqrt{3}\\ 1/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
The last equation of \(R\widehat{\mathbf{c}}=Q^T\mathbf{y}\) gives
\begin{equation*} \sqrt{2}\,\widehat c_1=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad \widehat c_1=\frac12. \end{equation*}
Substituting into the first equation gives
\begin{equation*} \sqrt{3}\,\widehat c_0+\sqrt{3}\left(\frac12\right) =\frac{5}{\sqrt{3}}, \end{equation*}
so
\begin{equation*} \widehat c_0=\frac53-\frac12=\frac76. \end{equation*}
🔗
Therefore
\begin{equation*} \widehat{\mathbf{c}}=\begin{bmatrix}7/6\\1/2\end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
and the fitted line is
\begin{equation*} y=\frac76+\frac12t. \end{equation*}
