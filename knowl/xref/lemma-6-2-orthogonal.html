Lemma 4.2.4. Expanding an orthonormal set.

Let \(\{\mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{q}_m\}\) be an orthonormal set in \(\R^n\text{.}\) Given \(\mathbf{x}\) in \(\R^n\text{,}\) define
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{x} - (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_1)\mathbf{q}_1 - (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_2)\mathbf{q}_2 - \cdots - (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_m)\mathbf{q}_m. \end{equation*}
Then:
  • \(\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{q}_k = 0\) for \(k = 1,2,\ldots,m\text{.}\)
    🔗
  • If \(\mathbf{x} \not \in \spans \{\mathbf{q}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{q}_m\}\text{,}\) then \(\mathbf{v} \neq \mathbf{0}\text{.}\) Normalize it by setting
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{q}_{m+1}=\frac{\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|}. \end{equation*}
    Then \(\{\mathbf{q}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{q}_m,\mathbf{q}_{m+1}\}\) is an orthonormal set.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Why is this true?.

Let \(c_i = \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{q}_i\) for each \(i\text{.}\) Given \(1 \leq k \leq m\text{,}\) we calculate that
\begin{align*} \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{q}_k \amp = (\mathbf{x} - c_1\mathbf{q}_1 - c_2\mathbf{q}_2 - \cdots - c_m\mathbf{q}_m) \cdot \mathbf{q}_k\\ \amp = \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{q}_k - c_1(\mathbf{q}_1 \cdot \mathbf{q}_k) - \cdots - c_k(\mathbf{q}_k \cdot \mathbf{q}_k) - \cdots - c_m(\mathbf{q}_m \cdot \mathbf{q}_k) \end{align*}
Since the set is orthonormal, \(\mathbf{q}_i \cdot \mathbf{q}_k = 0\) for \(i \neq k\) and \(\mathbf{q}_k \cdot \mathbf{q}_k=1\text{.}\) Thus
\begin{align*} \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{q}_k \amp = \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{q}_k - c_k\\ \amp = \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{q}_k - \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{q}_k = 0 \end{align*}
This proves the first property. If \(\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}\text{,}\) then \(\mathbf{x}=c_1\mathbf{q}_1+\cdots+c_m\mathbf{q}_m\) lies in the original span. Therefore, if \(\mathbf{x}\) is outside that span, \(\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{0}\) and we can normalize it. The vector \(\mathbf{q}_{m+1}=\mathbf{v}/\|\mathbf{v}\|\) has norm one and remains orthogonal to every \(\mathbf{q}_k\text{,}\) so the enlarged set is orthonormal.
