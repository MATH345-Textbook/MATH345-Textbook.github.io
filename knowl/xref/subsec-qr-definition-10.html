Theorem 4.4.2. Gram–Schmidt produces \(QR\).

Every \(m\times n\) matrix \(A\) with linearly independent columns has a reduced \(QR\) factorization
\begin{equation*} A=QR \end{equation*}
in which the columns of \(Q\) are orthonormal and \(R\) is upper triangular with positive diagonal entries.
🔗
🔗

Why is this true?.

Apply the Gram–Schmidt algorithm 4.2.9 to the columns
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{a}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{a}_n. \end{equation*}
🔗
At stage \(j\text{,}\) the algorithm gives
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{a}_j = \sum_{i\leq j} r_{ij}\mathbf{q}_i. \end{equation*}
🔗
Collecting these equations column by column gives
\begin{equation*} A=QR. \end{equation*}
🔗
The columns of \(Q\) are orthonormal. The condition \(i\leq j\) makes \(R\) upper triangular, and
\begin{equation*} r_{jj} = \|\mathbf{v}_j\| > 0 \end{equation*}
because the input columns are linearly independent.
