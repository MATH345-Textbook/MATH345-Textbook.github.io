Activity 3.3.36. Building a gradient and Hessian in SymPy (U3-LO3).
Collect the derivatives of \(f(x,y)=3x^2y+2x-3y\) into a vector and a matrix. As in Unit 2, sp.Matrix([a, b]) creates a column matrix and .shape reports its numbers of rows and columns. The command sp.hessian(f, (x, y)) constructs the Hessian with coordinates ordered as \(x,y\text{.}\)
🔗
import sympy as sp

x, y = sp.symbols("x y")
f = 3*x**2*y + 2*x - 3*y
grad_f = sp.Matrix([sp.diff(f, x), sp.diff(f, y)])
H = sp.hessian(f, (x, y))

print(grad_f)
print(H)
print((grad_f.shape, H.shape))
Output:

Matrix([[6*y, 6*x], [6*x, 0]])

Solution.
The gradient collects two first partial derivatives, while the Hessian collects the four second partial derivatives:
\begin{equation*} \nabla f(x,y)=\begin{bmatrix}6xy+2\\3x^2-3\end{bmatrix},\qquad H_f(x,y)=\begin{bmatrix}6y&6x\\6x&0\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
At the specified point,
\begin{equation*} \nabla f(1,2)=\begin{bmatrix}14\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad H_f(1,2)=\begin{bmatrix}12&6\\6&0\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Evaluating entries leaves the shapes \(2\times1\) and \(2\times2\) unchanged.
🔗
SymPy uses zero-based indices, so H[0, 1] is the first-row, second-column entry, \(f_{xy}=6x\text{.}\) The mixed partial derivatives agree because the polynomial satisfies Clairaut’s theorem. The zero entry is \(f_{yy}\text{:}\) \(f_y=3x^2-3\) is independent of \(y\text{,}\) but it is not the zero function.
