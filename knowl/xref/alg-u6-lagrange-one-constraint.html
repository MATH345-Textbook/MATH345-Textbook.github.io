Algorithm 6.2.5. Lagrange multipliers with one constraint.

Given differentiable scalar-valued functions \(f\) and \(g\text{,}\) to find candidates for extrema of \(f(\mathbf{x})\) subject to
\begin{equation*} g(\mathbf{x})=k: \end{equation*}
🔗
  1. Compute \(\nabla f\) and \(\nabla g\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Solve
    \begin{equation*} \nabla f(\mathbf{x})=\lambda\nabla g(\mathbf{x}) \end{equation*}
    together with
    \begin{equation*} g(\mathbf{x})=k. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Evaluate \(f\) at every candidate.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Compare the values when absolute extrema are requested.
    🔗
    🔗
If \(\nabla g(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{0}\) at a feasible point, Theorem 6.2.3 does not apply there, so check that point separately. When the constraint describes one boundary piece of a larger region, combine these candidates with the other candidates required by Algorithm 6.1.2.
