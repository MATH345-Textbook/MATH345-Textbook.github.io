Additional exercise 4.6.1. Orthogonal coordinates and projection.

Let
🔗
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{q}_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1\\ 1\\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{q}_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1\\ -1\\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} Q = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{q}_1\amp\mathbf{q}_2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 3\\ 1\\ 2 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Use “Projection and residual 4.2.5” as a geometric guide.
🔗
  1. Check that
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} Q^TQ=I_2. \end{equation*}
    🔗
  2. Compute the coordinate vector
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{c}=Q^T\mathbf{x}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
  3. Compute
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \widehat{\mathbf{x}}=Q\mathbf{c}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
  4. Compute the residual
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{r} = \mathbf{x}-\widehat{\mathbf{x}}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
  5. Check that
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} Q^T\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{0}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
  6. Explain how the calculation follows the pattern “score, then combine.”
    🔗
    🔗
  7. Which vector is the projection of \(\mathbf{x}\text{:}\) \(\mathbf{c}\) or \(\widehat{\mathbf{x}}\text{?}\)
