Theorem 4.1.5. Expansion theorem.

Let \(\{\mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{q}_m\}\) be an orthonormal basis of a subspace \(U\) of \(\R^n\text{.}\) If \(\mathbf{x}\) is any vector in \(U\text{,}\) we have
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_1)\mathbf{q}_1 + (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_2)\mathbf{q}_2 + \cdots + (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_m)\mathbf{q}_m. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Why is this true?.

Since \(\{\mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{q}_m\}\) is a basis of \(U\text{,}\) any \(\mathbf{x} \in U\) can be written uniquely as
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{x} = c_1\mathbf{q}_1 + c_2\mathbf{q}_2 + \cdots + c_m\mathbf{q}_m \end{equation*}
for some \(c_1,\dots,c_m\text{.}\) To find coefficient \(c_1\text{,}\) take the dot product of both sides with \(\mathbf{q}_1\text{,}\) obtaining that
\begin{align*} \mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{q}_1 \amp = (c_1\mathbf{q}_1 + c_2\mathbf{q}_2 + \cdots + c_m\mathbf{q}_m) \cdot \mathbf{q}_1\\ \amp = c_1(\mathbf{q}_1 \cdot \mathbf{q}_1) + c_2(\mathbf{q}_2 \cdot \mathbf{q}_1) + \cdots + c_m(\mathbf{q}_m \cdot \mathbf{q}_1)\text{.} \end{align*}
Since the basis is orthonormal, \(\mathbf{q}_i \cdot \mathbf{q}_j = 0\) for \(i \neq j\) and \(\mathbf{q}_1\cdot\mathbf{q}_1=1\text{,}\) so the equation simplifies to
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_1=c_1. \end{equation*}
A similar argument shows that
\begin{equation*} c_i=\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_i \end{equation*}
for each \(i\text{,}\) concluding the proof.
