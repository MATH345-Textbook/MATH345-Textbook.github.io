Lemma 5.3.3.

Let \(Q\) be an \(n \times n\) orthogonal matrix. Then:
  1. \(Q\) preserves dot products, i.e., for any vectors \(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w} \in \R^n\text{,}\) \((Q\mathbf{v}) \cdot (Q\mathbf{w}) = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}\text{.}\)
    🔗
  2. \(Q\) preserves lengths, i.e., for any vector \(\mathbf{v} \in \R^n\text{,}\) \(\|Q\mathbf{v}\| = \|\mathbf{v}\|\text{.}\)
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Why is this true?.

Let \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{w}\) be column vectors. Recalling the fifth property of Theorem 1.4.4, we calculate that
\begin{align*} (Q\mathbf{v}) \cdot (Q\mathbf{w}) \amp = (Q\mathbf{v})^T(Q\mathbf{w})\\ \amp = \mathbf{v}^T Q^T Q \mathbf{w}\\ \amp = \mathbf{v}^T I_n \mathbf{w}\\ \amp = \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{w}\\ \amp = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}\text{.} \end{align*}
Thus \(Q\) preserves dot products.
🔗
Next, using that \(Q\) preserves dot products, and also Note 1.1.16, we find
\begin{equation*} \| Q\mathbf{v} \|^2 = (Q \mathbf{v}) \cdot (Q \mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \| \mathbf{v} \|^2\text{.} \end{equation*}
Taking square roots on both sides of this equation gives \(\|Q\mathbf{v}\| = \|\mathbf{v}\|\text{.}\)
