Activity 3.5.19. Differentiating a loss through its predictions (U3-LO6).
The squared-error loss in Activity 3.1.9 is a composition:
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{w}\longmapsto R(\mathbf{w})=X\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y} \longmapsto q(R(\mathbf{w})),\qquad q(\mathbf{r})=\mathbf{r}^T\mathbf{r}. \end{equation*}
Thus \(\mathcal L=q\circ R\text{.}\) The fixed data are still \(X=\begin{bmatrix}1\amp0\\0\amp1\\1\amp1\end{bmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{y}=(1,2,2)\text{.}\)
🔗
  1. Find \(J_R(\mathbf{w})\) and \(J_q(\mathbf{r})\text{,}\) including their shapes. Which map is affine, and which is nonlinear?
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Use the matrix chain rule to obtain \(J_{\mathcal L}(\mathbf{w})\text{,}\) and transpose it to obtain \(\nabla\mathcal L(\mathbf{w})\text{.}\) Evaluate at \(\mathbf{w}_0=(1,1)\) and compare with Activity 3.3.37.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Let \(\mathbf{w}(t)=(1,1)+t(1,1)\text{.}\) Find \(\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal L(\mathbf{w}(t))\) using the chain rule. Check by first substituting into the loss and then differentiating the resulting one-variable polynomial.
    🔗
    🔗
Solution.
  1. The residual map \(R:\R^2\to\R^3\) is affine, with
    \begin{equation*} J_R=X=\begin{bmatrix}1\amp0\\0\amp1\\1\amp1\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Its shape is \(3\times2\text{.}\) The squared-length function \(q:\R^3\to\R\) is nonlinear, with \(J_q(\mathbf{r})=2\mathbf{r}^T\text{,}\) a \(1\times3\) row.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Evaluate the outer derivative at the residual, then multiply:
    \begin{align*} J_{\mathcal L}(\mathbf{w})\amp=2(X\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y})^TX,\\ \nabla\mathcal L(\mathbf{w})\amp=2X^T(X\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{y}). \end{align*}
    The row product has shape \((1\times3)(3\times2)=1\times2\text{;}\) the gradient is a \(2\times1\) column. At \(\mathbf{w}_0\text{,}\) the residual is \((0,-1,0)\text{,}\) so
    \begin{equation*} \nabla\mathcal L(\mathbf{w}_0) =2\begin{bmatrix}1\amp0\amp1\\0\amp1\amp1\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}0\\-1\\0\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}0\\-2\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    This agrees with differentiating the expanded loss. Each gradient component combines the errors from the observations using the corresponding feature column.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The chain rule along the parameter line gives
    \begin{align*} \amp\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal L(\mathbf{w}(t))\\ \amp=\nabla\mathcal L(1+t,1+t)^T\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\\ \amp=\begin{bmatrix}6t\amp-2+6t\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\\ \amp=-2+12t. \end{align*}
    Direct substitution gives \(\mathcal L(1+t,1+t)=1-2t+6t^2\text{,}\) whose derivative is the same. At \(t=0\text{,}\) the rate is \(-2\text{;}\) multiplying by a small parameter change \(t=1/10\) recovers the predicted loss change \(-1/5\) from Activity 3.4.7.
