Theorem 1.3.3. Row dot-product view.

If \(A\) is an \(m\times n\) matrix with rows \(\operatorname{row}_1(A),\ldots,\operatorname{row}_m(A)\) as \(n\)-vectors, then
\begin{equation*} A\mathbf{x}= \begin{bmatrix} \operatorname{row}_1(A)\cdot\mathbf{x}\\ \operatorname{row}_2(A)\cdot\mathbf{x}\\ \vdots\\ \operatorname{row}_m(A)\cdot\mathbf{x} \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Why is this true?.

Write \(A=[\mathbf{a}_1\ \cdots\ \mathbf{a}_n]\text{,}\) where \(\mathbf{a}_j=(a_{1j},\ldots,a_{mj})\text{,}\) and let \(\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\text{.}\) Since
\begin{equation*} A\mathbf{x}=x_1\mathbf{a}_1+\cdots+x_n\mathbf{a}_n, \end{equation*}
its \(i\)-th entry is
\begin{equation*} x_1a_{i1}+\cdots+x_na_{in}=\operatorname{row}_i(A)\cdot\mathbf{x}. \end{equation*}
Stacking these entries gives the stated formula.
