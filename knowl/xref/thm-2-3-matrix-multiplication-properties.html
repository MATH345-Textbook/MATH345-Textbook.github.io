Theorem 1.4.4. Properties of matrix multiplication.

Assume that \(A,B,C\) are matrices of appropriate sizes and \(r\) is a scalar. Then
  1. \(A(BC)=(AB)C\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \((A+B)C=AC+BC\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(C(A+B)=CA+CB\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \(A(rB)=r(AB)=(rA)B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  5. \((AB)^T=B^TA^T\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Why is this true?.

We prove property 5. Let \(A\) be \(m\times n\) and \(B\) be \(n\times k\text{.}\) Both \((AB)^T\) and \(B^TA^T\) have shape \(k\times m\text{.}\) Transposition exchanges row and column indices, so the entry formula in Theorem 1.4.2 gives
\begin{align*} ((AB)^T)_{ij} \amp=(AB)_{ji}\\ \amp=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{j\ell}b_{\ell i}\\ \amp=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}b_{\ell i}a_{j\ell}\\ \amp=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}(B^T)_{i\ell}(A^T)_{\ell j}\\ \amp=(B^TA^T)_{ij}. \end{align*}
We can reverse the two scalar factors in each summand because real-number multiplication is commutative. Every corresponding entry agrees, so \((AB)^T=B^TA^T\text{.}\)
