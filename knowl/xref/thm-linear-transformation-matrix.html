Theorem 1.3.18. Every linear map has a matrix.

Every linear map \(T:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m\) is given by multiplication by a unique matrix. The columns of that matrix are \(T(\mathbf{e}_1),\ldots,T(\mathbf{e}_n)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Why is this true?.

Write \(\mathbf{x}=x_1\mathbf{e}_1+\cdots+x_n\mathbf{e}_n\) and let \(A=[\,T(\mathbf{e}_1)\ \cdots\ T(\mathbf{e}_n)\,]\text{.}\) By linearity and the column view of matrix-vector multiplication,
\begin{equation*} T(\mathbf{x})=x_1T(\mathbf{e}_1)+\cdots+x_nT(\mathbf{e}_n)=A\mathbf{x}. \end{equation*}
This matrix is unique because its \(j\)-th column must be \(A\mathbf{e}_j=T(\mathbf{e}_j)\text{.}\)
