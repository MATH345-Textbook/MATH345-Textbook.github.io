Theorem 4.2.8. Projection theorem.

Let \(U\) be a subspace of \(\R^n\) with orthonormal basis \(\{\mathbf{q}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{q}_m\}\text{.}\) For any vector \(\mathbf{x} \in \R^n\text{,}\) define
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{p} = (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_1)\mathbf{q}_1 + (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_2)\mathbf{q}_2 + \cdots + (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_m)\mathbf{q}_m. \end{equation*}
Then \(\mathbf{p}=\proj_U(\mathbf{x})\text{.}\) The difference
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{v}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{x}-\proj_U(\mathbf{x}) \end{equation*}
is called the residual and lies in \(U^\perp\text{.}\) In particular, if \(\mathbf{x}\) is already in \(U\text{,}\) then \(\mathbf{x}\) equals \(\mathbf{p}\text{.}\)

Why is this true?.

If \(\{ \mathbf{q}_1, \dots, \mathbf{q}_m \}\) is an orthonormal basis for \(U\text{,}\) and we define
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{p} = (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_1)\mathbf{q}_1 + (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_2)\mathbf{q}_2 + \cdots + (\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{q}_m)\mathbf{q}_m, \end{equation*}
then \(\mathbf{p}\) is a linear combination of the basis vectors, so \(\mathbf{p}\in U\text{.}\) The first conclusion of Lemma 4.2.4 shows that \(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}\) is orthogonal to every basis vector, so \(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}\in U^\perp\text{.}\) Thus if \(\mathbf{z} \in U\) and \(\mathbf{z} \neq \mathbf{p}\text{,}\) the Pythagorean theorem (Theorem 4.1.3) tells us that
\begin{align*} \| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{z} \|^2 \amp = \| (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}) + (\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{z}) \|^2\\ \amp = \| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p} \|^2 + \| \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{z} \|^2\text{.} \end{align*}
Since \(\mathbf{p} \neq \mathbf{z}\text{,}\) we find that
\begin{equation*} \| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{z} \|^2 \gt \| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p} \|^2\text{,} \end{equation*}
and taking square roots gives that
\begin{equation*} \| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{z} \| \gt \| \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p} \|\text{.} \end{equation*}
Thus the vector \(\mathbf{p}\) we have defined is equal to \(\proj_U(\mathbf{x})\text{.}\) Since we have already verified that \(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p} = \mathbf{x} - \proj_U(\mathbf{x})\) is in \(U^\perp\text{,}\) the theorem follows.
