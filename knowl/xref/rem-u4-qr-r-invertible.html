Remark 4.4.2. Why \(R\) is invertible.

For the Gram–Schmidt convention above, \(R\) is upper triangular with positive diagonal entries. By Theorem 2.5.37,
\begin{equation*} \det(R)=r_{11}\cdots r_{nn}>0. \end{equation*}
Thus \(\det(R)\ne 0\text{,}\) so \(R\) is invertible by Theorem 2.5.41.
