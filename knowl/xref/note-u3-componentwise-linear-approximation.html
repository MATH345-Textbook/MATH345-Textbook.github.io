Note 3.4.18. Componentwise linear approximation.

Recall from Jacobian matrices that \(J_F(\mathbf{a})\) is the \(m\times n\) matrix whose \((i,j)\)-entry is \(\frac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_j}(\mathbf{a})\text{.}\) Its \(i\)th row is the transpose of the gradient of the component function \(F_i\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_1}(\mathbf{a}) \amp \cdots \amp \dfrac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_n}(\mathbf{a}) \end{bmatrix} = (\nabla F_i)(\mathbf{a})^T. \end{equation*}
🔗
Multiplying this row by \(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}\) gives the \(i\)th component of \(J_F(\mathbf{a})(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a})\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} L_i(\mathbf{x}) = F_i(\mathbf{a}) + (\nabla F_i)(\mathbf{a})^T(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}) = F_i(\mathbf{a}) + (\nabla F_i)(\mathbf{a})\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{a}). \end{equation*}
Thus the components of \(L\) are precisely the scalar-valued linear approximations of the components of \(F\text{.}\)
