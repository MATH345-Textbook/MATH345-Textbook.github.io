Theorem 1.4.2.

If \(A\) is \(m\times n\) and \(B\) is \(n\times k\text{,}\) then the \((i,j)\)-entry of \(AB\) is the dot product of row \(i\) of \(A\) with column \(j\) of \(B\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} (AB)_{ij}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{i\ell}b_{\ell j}. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Why is this true?.

By the definition of matrix multiplication, column \(j\) of \(AB\) is \(A\mathbf{b}_j\text{,}\) where \(\mathbf{b}_j=(b_{1j},\ldots,b_{nj})\) is column \(j\) of \(B\text{.}\) The row dot-product rule for matrix-vector multiplication in Theorem 1.3.3 therefore gives
\begin{align*} (AB)_{ij} \amp=(A\mathbf{b}_j)_i\\ \amp=\operatorname{row}_i(A)\cdot\mathbf{b}_j\\ \amp=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}a_{i\ell}b_{\ell j}. \end{align*}
