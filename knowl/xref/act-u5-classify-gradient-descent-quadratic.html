Activity 5.5.12.

Consider the function
\begin{equation*} f(x,y)=x^2-3xy+3y^2+5y+2x. \end{equation*}
Find all critical points of \(f\) and classify each as a local minimum, a local maximum, or a saddle point.
🔗
Solution.
We find the critical points by solving \(\nabla f(x,y)=\mathbf{0}\text{.}\) Since
\begin{equation*} f_x(x,y)=2x-3y+2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad f_y(x,y)=-3x+6y+5, \end{equation*}
the critical points satisfy
\begin{equation*} 2x-3y+2=0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad -3x+6y+5=0. \end{equation*}
The first equation gives \(x=(3y-2)/2\text{.}\) Substituting this expression into the second equation gives
\begin{align*} -3\left(\frac{3y-2}{2}\right)+6y+5 &=0,\\ \frac{3y}{2} &=-8,\\ y &=-\frac{16}{3}. \end{align*}
It follows that
\begin{equation*} x=\frac{3(-16/3)-2}{2}=-9, \end{equation*}
so the only critical point is \((-9,-16/3)\text{.}\)
🔗
The Hessian matrix is the constant matrix
\begin{equation*} H_f(x,y)= \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ -3 & 6 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Thus the discriminant is
\begin{equation*} D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2=(2)(6)-(-3)^2=3>0. \end{equation*}
Since \(D>0\) and \(f_{xx}=2>0\text{,}\) the second derivative test tells us that \((-9,-16/3)\) is a local minimum of \(f\text{.}\)
