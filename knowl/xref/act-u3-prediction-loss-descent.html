Activity 3.6.26. Improving a model’s predictions with one step (U3-LO8, U3-LO9).

Return to the three observations from Activity 3.1.9 and the loss
\begin{equation*} \mathcal L(w_1,w_2)=(w_1-1)^2+(w_2-2)^2+(w_1+w_2-2)^2. \end{equation*}
At the starting weights \(\mathbf{w}_0=(1,1)\text{,}\) the loss is \(1\) and the gradient is \((0,-2)\text{.}\) In this model, a training step adjusts the weights while the features \(X\) and observations \(\mathbf{y}\) remain fixed.
🔗
  1. Find the unit direction of greatest decrease at \(\mathbf{w}_0\) and its directional derivative. Take one gradient-descent step with learning rate \(\alpha=1/10\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Compute the new prediction vector, residual, and loss. Compare the actual change with the first-order prediction \(-\alpha\|\nabla\mathcal L(\mathbf{w}_0)\|^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Evaluate the gradient at the new weights. Has the step reached a critical point?
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Starting again at \(\mathbf{w}_0\text{,}\) what happens if \(\alpha=1\text{?}\) Explain why the same descent direction can give an increase in loss.
    🔗
    🔗
Solution.
  1. The negative gradient is \((0,2)\text{,}\) so its unit direction is \((0,1)\text{.}\) The directional derivative is \((0,-2)\cdot(0,1)=-2\text{.}\) Gradient descent uses the unnormalized gradient:
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{w}_1 =\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix} -\frac{1}{10}\begin{bmatrix}0\\-2\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}1\\6/5\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The new predictions and residuals are
    \begin{equation*} X\mathbf{w}_1=\begin{bmatrix}1\\6/5\\11/5\end{bmatrix}, \qquad R(\mathbf{w}_1)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\-4/5\\1/5\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    The loss is \(16/25+1/25=17/25=0.68\text{,}\) down from \(1\text{.}\) The predicted change is \(-(1/10)(4)=-2/5\text{,}\) while the actual change is \(-8/25\text{.}\) Their difference, \(2/25\text{,}\) is the squared length of \(X(\mathbf{w}_1-\mathbf{w}_0)=(0,1/5,1/5)\text{,}\) as in Activity 3.4.7.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. The gradient formula from Activity 3.5.21 gives
    \begin{equation*} \nabla\mathcal L(\mathbf{w}_1) =2X^T\begin{bmatrix}0\\-4/5\\1/5\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}2/5\\-6/5\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    It is nonzero, so the new weights are not a critical point. The step improves the fit without finishing the minimization.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. With \(\alpha=1\text{,}\) the new weights are \((1,3)\text{,}\) the predictions are \((1,3,4)\text{,}\) and the residual is \((0,1,2)\text{.}\) The loss becomes \(5\text{,}\) which is larger than the starting value. The negative gradient specifies a direction of local decrease; the learning rate determines how far we travel. A step that is too large can increase the loss, just as in Activity 3.6.23.
