Definition 6.4.2. First principal direction.

A first principal direction is a unit vector \(\mathbf{v}_1\) that maximizes
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{v}^TC\mathbf{v} \end{equation*}
subject to
\begin{equation*} \|\mathbf{v}\|=1. \end{equation*}
The entries of
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{t}_1=Z\mathbf{v}_1 \end{equation*}
are the first principal-component scores.
🔗
By Fact 6.3.1, \(\mathbf{v}_1\) may be chosen as a unit eigenvector of \(C\) corresponding to its largest eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\text{.}\) The maximum captured variation is \(\lambda_1\text{.}\)
