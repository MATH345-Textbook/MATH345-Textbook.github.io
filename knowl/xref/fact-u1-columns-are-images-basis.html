Fact 1.3.15.
If \(A=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{a}_1 \amp \cdots \amp \mathbf{a}_n\end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) then \(A\mathbf{e}_j=\mathbf{a}_j\text{.}\) Applying a matrix to a standard basis vector picks out a column.
🔗
🔗
Why is this true?.
The coordinates of \(\mathbf{e}_j\) are all zero except for a \(1\) in position \(j\text{.}\) Thus the column formula for the matrix-vector product gives
\begin{equation*} A\mathbf{e}_j=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\mathbf{e}_j)_k\mathbf{a}_k=\mathbf{a}_j. \end{equation*}
Here \((\mathbf{e}_j)_k\) denotes the \(k\)-th coordinate of \(\mathbf{e}_j\text{,}\) which equals \(1\) when \(k=j\) and \(0\) otherwise.
