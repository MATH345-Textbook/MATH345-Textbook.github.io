Fact 6.6.1. PCA from a centered-data SVD.

Let
\begin{equation*} Z\in\R^{n\times d} \end{equation*}
be a centered data matrix, and let
\begin{equation*} Z=U\Sigma V^T \end{equation*}
be an SVD. Write
\begin{equation*} V= \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{v}_1 \amp \cdots \amp \mathbf{v}_d \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
and include zero singular values when needed. Then
\begin{equation*} C=\frac1nZ^TZ = V\left(\frac1n\Sigma^T\Sigma\right)V^T. \end{equation*}
🔗
Therefore:
🔗
  • the right singular vectors \(\mathbf{v}_j\) are principal directions;
    🔗
    🔗
  • the covariance eigenvalues are
    \begin{equation*} \lambda_j=\frac{\sigma_j^2}{n}; \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  • the principal-coordinate matrix is
    \begin{equation*} T=ZV=U\Sigma. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
In particular, the \(j\)-th score vector is
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{t}_j=\sigma_j\mathbf{u}_j \end{equation*}
when \(\sigma_j>0\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Why is this true?.

Since \(U\) is orthogonal,
\begin{equation*} U^TU=I. \end{equation*}
Therefore
\begin{align*} Z^TZ\\ \amp= (U\Sigma V^T)^T(U\Sigma V^T)\\ \amp= V\Sigma^TU^TU\Sigma V^T\\ \amp= V\Sigma^T\Sigma V^T. \end{align*}
Dividing by \(n\) gives the displayed orthogonal diagonalization of \(C\text{.}\) The columns of \(V\) are therefore covariance eigenvectors, with eigenvalues \(\sigma_j^2/n\text{.}\)
🔗
Also,
\begin{align*} ZV\\ \amp= U\Sigma V^TV\\ \amp= U\Sigma. \end{align*}
Thus the columns of \(U\Sigma\) are the principal-component score vectors.
