Algorithm 6.2.8. Lagrange multipliers with several constraints.

To find candidates for extrema of \(f(\mathbf{x})\) subject to
\begin{equation*} g_1(\mathbf{x})=k_1,\quad\ldots,\quad g_m(\mathbf{x})=k_m: \end{equation*}
🔗
  1. Compute
    \begin{equation*} \nabla f,\quad\nabla g_1,\quad\ldots,\quad\nabla g_m. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Solve
    \begin{equation*} \nabla f = \lambda_1\nabla g_1+\cdots+\lambda_m\nabla g_m \end{equation*}
    together with all \(m\) constraint equations.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Evaluate \(f\) at every candidate.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Compare the values when absolute extrema are requested.
    🔗
    🔗
If the constraint gradients are linearly dependent at a feasible point, the theorem does not apply there. Check such points separately.
